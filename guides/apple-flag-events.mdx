---
title: "Handling Apple flag events"
description: "What line.apple_id_flagged means, how to recover, what to tell the end-user."
---

iMessage delivery depends on Apple keeping the line's Apple ID in good
standing. If Apple flags the account — usually for sending patterns it
considers suspicious — iMessage stops working immediately. iSnap surfaces
this via the `line.apple_id_flagged` webhook so you can react fast.

This page covers what to do on each end of the SKU split.

## Why it happens

Apple's iMessage flag heuristics aren't documented, but iSnap's signal
in the field comes down to:

- **Sending patterns that look automated** — bursts, regular cadence,
  identical bodies across many recipients.
- **Low reply rate** — Apple's most reliable bot signal. The
  [warming model](/concepts/quotas-and-warming) and the new/known
  contact split exist precisely to keep this ratio healthy.
- **Aggressive opt-out reports** — recipients tapping "Report Junk"
  enough times.

Hit any of these and the Apple ID becomes "flagged." From iSnap's
side, `iMessage` sends start failing. The line itself is still alive —
SMS / RCS still work. iMessage is the only channel that breaks.

## The webhook

```json
{
  "id": "evt_01HX...",
  "type": "line.apple_id_flagged",
  "api_version": "1.1.0",
  "created_at": "2026-05-05T14:32:00Z",
  "data": {
    "line": {
      "id": "line_aBc...",
      "billing_tier": "rental_iphone",
      "byod": false,
      "phone_number": "+15559998888"
    },
    "detected_at": "2026-05-05T14:31:55Z"
  }
}
```

This event fires for **both rental and BYOD** lines — Apple flags are
durable and customer-actionable (pricing tier doesn't matter), so they're
not in the rental-suppression list.

## Recovery

### Rental lines

iSnap ops handles the recovery. You'll receive a follow-up event when
the line is back:

- `line.apple_id_recovered` (Phase 2 doc — TODO) once the iSnap operator
  re-auths the Apple ID.

While the line is flagged, route iMessage traffic away from it (your
fallback chain) or pause that customer's outbound. Don't keep retrying
— Apple will hold the flag longer if traffic keeps coming.

Typical recovery time: **2–24 hours**. Severe cases (multiple flags
in a week) may require Apple ID rotation, which iSnap operators handle.

### BYOD lines

The customer owns the Apple ID, so the customer has to re-auth. Send
them through the bridge UI:

1. Surface the flag in your dashboard with clear copy:
   *"iMessage on +1 555… is temporarily unavailable. Re-auth your Apple
   ID via the bridge to restore it."*
2. The bridge presents Apple's re-auth flow (web view → 2FA on the
   trusted device).
3. Once re-auth succeeds, the bridge tells iSnap and a follow-up event
   fires.

The **bridge SDK** ships a deep link — your dashboard can open the
bridge's re-auth screen directly via
`isnap-bridge://auth/reauth?line_id=line_aBc...` (Phase 2 doc — TODO).

## What to tell the end-user

Apple flags are user-actionable in the BYOD case, so the messaging
matters. Three things to communicate:

1. **What's broken.** "iMessage is temporarily unavailable for this
   line." Don't say "your account is flagged" — that reads like a moral
   judgment. It's not.
2. **What still works.** SMS and RCS keep going. If your fallback
   chain handles the swap automatically, lead with that.
3. **What they need to do.** For BYOD, link the bridge re-auth flow.
   For rental, "iSnap is on it; expected back within 24h" is honest.

## Suppress the alert noise

Don't panic-email your ops team on every `line.apple_id_flagged`.
Production-grade integrations:

- **Group by line.** A single flag doesn't deserve 15 escalations.
- **Suppress within a 24h window** — once notified, second + third
  flags on the same line in the same window get folded.
- **Surface persistent flags** (>72h without recovery) as ops
  escalation, not first-flag noise.

## Prevention beats recovery

The warming model and the 5-bucket quota gate exist to make flags
rare. If you see a flag, audit:

- Was the line warmed? See `GET /v1/lines/{id}/config`'s `warming_state`.
- Was the new/known contact ratio out of whack? Reply rate &lt; 30% is a
  red flag.
- Was traffic bursty? The hourly red-zone aggregator
  (`bucket: hourly_chronic`) flags this before Apple does.

[Concepts → Quotas + warming](/concepts/quotas-and-warming) covers
the prevention side end-to-end.
