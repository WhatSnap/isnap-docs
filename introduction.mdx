iSnap is a Hardware-as-a-Service telephony platform. Real iPhones, real
Macs, real Androids — exposed through a single API so your messages
originate from real handsets with real SIMs. That's how iSnap reaches
recipients that bulk-shaped traffic from generic A2P providers can't.

## Three line types

You'll pick one (or mix — they coexist on the same account):

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Rental iMessage line — ~$250 / mo" icon="apple">
    iSnap provisions and operates a Mac mini and iPhone for you. The
    hardware lives in our data center; you consume the line via API.
    Best when you want zero device management.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Rental Android line — ~$175 / mo" icon="android">
    iSnap provisions and operates an Android handset on a carrier SIM.
    SMS and RCS native. Same "we own the hardware" model as the
    iMessage rental.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="BYOD bridge — ~$15 / mo per device" icon="link">
    Bring your own Mac + iPhone, or your own Android. Install the iSnap
    bridge, pair with an activation code, and the line shows up in your
    account. Same API, much lower cost — at the price of you owning the
    hardware reliability.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Channels

iSnap covers **iMessage**, **SMS**, and **RCS**. On a send, you pick a
`channel` (`auto | iMessage | SMS | RCS`); on the response and on
inbound webhooks, the `service` field reports which one was actually
used. RCS is a capability of the Android line and the modern fallback
target for `iMessage → RCS → SMS`, not a separate line type.

## What iSnap is not

- **Not a CRM.** Messages are retained for 72 hours. Attachments live
  on short-lived signed URLs. Persist your own conversation history.
- **Not a marketing blast tool.** Real-device delivery is high-trust
  by design; the warming and quota model exists to keep it that way.
  Volumes are capped by line health, not by your plan tier.
