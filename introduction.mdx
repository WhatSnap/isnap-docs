iSnap is a Hardware-as-a-Service telephony platform. We rent (or pair) physical
phones — iPhones, Macs, and Androids — to your business through an API, so your
messages originate from real handsets with real SIMs. That's how iSnap bypasses
the A2P carrier filters that stop bulk-shaped traffic from generic CPaaS vendors.

If you came here looking for "Twilio with better deliverability," you're in the
right place. If you need cloud A2P at scale, [Twilio](https://twilio.com),
[MessageBird](https://messagebird.com), and [Telnyx](https://telnyx.com) are the
right tools — iSnap is a different product class.

## Three SKUs

You'll pick one (or mix — they coexist on the same account, same API, same
billing relationship):

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Rental iMessage line — ~$250 / mo" icon="apple">
    iSnap provisions a Mac mini and an iPhone, runs them in our farm, and
    you consume the line via API. The hardware is ours; you never see it.
    Best for businesses that want zero device management.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Rental Android line — ~$175 / mo" icon="android">
    iSnap provisions an Android handset on a carrier SIM, in our farm.
    SMS and RCS native. Same "we own the hardware" model.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="BYOD bridge — ~$15 / mo per device" icon="link">
    You bring your own Mac + iPhone, or your own Android. You install the
    iSnap bridge app, pair with an activation code, and the line shows up
    in your account. Same API, same webhooks, much lower cost — at the
    price of you owning the hardware reliability.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Channels — what's in, what's not

iSnap covers **iMessage**, **SMS**, and **RCS**. The `service` enum on every
message is one of `iMessage | SMS | RCS`. RCS is a capability of the Android
line (and the modern fallback target for `iMessage → RCS → SMS`), not a
separate SKU.

**WhatsApp is not in scope.** It's a separate ecosystem (Meta policies,
Business API partners, opt-in flows) and would dilute the
P2P-with-real-devices pitch. If you need WhatsApp, use a Meta BSP.

## What iSnap is *not*

- **Not a CRM.** We retain messages for 72 hours max. Attachments live on
  short-lived signed URLs. You persist your own conversation history.
- **Not a marketing blast tool.** Real-device delivery is high-trust by
  design; the warming and quota model exists to keep it that way. If you
  need 100k SMS/hour, this isn't the platform.
- **Not vendor-locked to one partner.** WhatSnap was the first iSnap
  consumer, but the SDK and API are neutral — wholesale (one master
  account) and reseller (per-end-user accounts via OAuth) are both
  first-class.
